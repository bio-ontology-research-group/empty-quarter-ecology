## Methods

We partitioned the association of soil pH and transect position with community composition using 560 campaign–site–compartment groups from 60 sites. We summed genus counts within each group, retained groups with at least 2,000 reads, and selected the 200 most abundant genera among those present in at least 20% of groups. We applied a centred log-ratio transformation after adding 0.5 to each count. Four weighted multivariate linear models fitted campaign-by-compartment categories alone, categories with pH, categories with linear and quadratic transect terms, and all predictors together. Each site's groups had total weight one. Differences in residual sums of squares identified the unique pH, unique geographic and shared association fractions, each divided by the residual sum of squares of the common category-only model.

To describe the geographic pattern of average site composition, we also adjusted the CLR response, pH and both geographic columns against the same category design and averaged their residuals within each site. We partitioned the resulting 60-site response using the adjusted site-average predictors and a common response denominator. We assessed sampling sensitivity with 1,999 whole-site bootstrap draws and 1,999 draws for each of three non-circular contiguous-block schemes containing 5, 10 or 15 sites per block. Each draw retained all groups from a selected site and re-estimated the category adjustment. Feature selection and transformation remained fixed. We report raw descriptive fractions and resampling distributions, with exact inputs, software versions, commands and all replicate results provided in the accompanying analysis package.

## Results

pH and geographic position shared an association with average community composition. In the 60-site partition, unique pH, unique geography and their shared component accounted for 1.38%, 13.56% and 23.86% of variation in the adjusted site-average CLR response, respectively; 61.20% remained unexplained. The corresponding fractions across all 560 groups, including within-site variation and weighting each site equally, were 0.31%, 6.78% and 4.76%, with 88.15% unexplained. Adjusted geographic terms accounted for 84.4% of variation in adjusted site-average pH. The partition therefore places much of the pH-associated spatial pattern in the component shared with geography. Removing one site at a time kept the unique pH fraction between 1.25% and 1.63% for site averages. Spatial-block resampling produced wider changes in the shared component, reflecting dependence on the sampled portion of the transect.

## Discussion

The pH analysis estimates conditional community associations within the environmental gradients represented by this transect. The substantial covariance of pH with geographic position limits independent variation available for estimating their separate contributions. Archived-soil measurements were linked to community profiles at the campaign, site and compartment level. Replicated pH manipulations or sampling that crosses pH levels with geographic position would provide additional leverage for estimating causal effects and for evaluating variation among matched physical specimens.
